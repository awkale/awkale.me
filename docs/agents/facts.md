## The concert archive

* **The archive has no personal-participation dimension.** `concert` links conductor / hall / orchestra / season / program; `programItem` links composer / work / soloists. The only named people anywhere are **soloists and conductors** — section players are not recorded. "Which concerts did I play" must be supplied by hand.
* Three organizations: Brooklyn Symphony Orchestra (BSO), Brooklyn Heights Orchestra (BHO), Brooklyn Heights Music Society (BHMS). BSO is **Brooklyn**, not Boston.
* **The** `genre` **field is not a genre — it is the form word in the title**, produced by a first-match-wins regex, so it can only categorize works that name their own form. 104 of the 322 played works (32%) have none, and they are disproportionately the *canonical* repertoire (Boléro, Scheherazade, The Planets, Pines of Rome). Where it does fire it is ~6% wrong: `Aria` (13) is really "excerpt from a staged work", and `Ballet` holds 2 while ~15 ballets sit under `Suite`. **Retired as a concept** by [Decide the genre-coverage policy for my 348 works](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-13/decide-the-genre-coverage-policy-for-my-348-works).
* **The archive holds no diacritics.** Exactly one non-ASCII string (`Façade`) across 1,228 `composer` / `work` / `hall` / `soloist` / `conductor` records — it is `Dvorak`, `Saint-Saens`, `Bartok`, `Faure`, `Espana`, `Petroushka`. Alex wants them rendered, so displaying them is **restoring data that was never captured**, not a rendering choice. Slugs are unaffected (any derivation folds to ASCII). The IMSLP composer match returns canonical spellings free, so the `composer.period` seed is the cheapest moment to fix it — handed to [Decide where archive URL slugs come from](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-18/decide-where-archive-url-slugs-come-from).
* **IMSLP is a usable, confirmed data source.** It runs MediaWiki, so `imslp.org/api.php?action=query&prop=categories&titles=…` returns a work's `X style` and a composer's `People from the X era` category directly; a separate endpoint (`API.ISCR.php`, `type=1`) enumerates all **55,263** composer pages for offline matching. Two limits: composer-page coverage of the played repertoire is ~90% and the residual is living composers; and **work-title matching is lossy** — the archive says `Scheherazade` and `The Moldau` where IMSLP files `Shéhérazade` and `Má vlast`.
* **8 of 244 composers do hold hand-curated** `dateOfBirth` **and** `dateOfDeath` — Beethoven, Schubert, Rossini, Sibelius, Mahler, Richard Strauss, Kodály, Vaughan Williams. The importer's "never overwrites a non-empty field" property is confirmed empirically, so re-running it is safe. Corollary: a data fix that *must* overwrite cannot go through the importer.
* **Nobiliary particles live in** `lastName`**, so they break sorting and slugs — not display.** Beethoven is `firstName: "Ludwig"`, `lastName: "van Beethoven"`, `sortName: "van Beethoven, Ludwig"`; likewise `von Weber`, `de Falla`, `de Sarasate` (all in scope) and `von Suppe` (not). Display renders correctly; what breaks is that a slug derived from `sortName` or `lastName` gives `/concerts/composers/van-beethoven-ludwig`, filing Beethoven under **V**, and an A–Z index sorts four in-scope composers under v/v/d/d. Entry ids already carry it: `cmp-van-beethoven-ludwig`. This is a **second, independent contaminant** on `sortName` alongside the arranger text — fixing [Settle composer identity and how arrangements are modeled](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-15/settle-composer-identity-and-how-arrangements-are-modeled) does not fix it — **now confirmed, since that ticket resolved without touching** `lastName` **at all**; see [Decide where archive URL slugs come from](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-18/decide-where-archive-url-slugs-come-from). **Resolved** by that ticket via the **Dutch/European rule** — the prefix is *relocated* to the back rather than stripped (`Beethoven, Ludwig van`, filing under B), so nothing is discarded and the display name stays recoverable from `sortName` alone. It also found a **third** contaminant of the same kind: **honorifics**, which split Walton and Sullivan across two records each.
* **The BSO publishes its own performance video, and that is what makes recordings cheap.** Official YouTube channel [@BKLYNsymphony](https://www.youtube.com/@BKLYNsymphony), a 9-item [Video Performances](https://www.brooklynsymphonyorchestra.org/bso-video-performances) carousel, and a [SoundCloud](https://soundcloud.com/brooklyn-symphony-orch) feed that is a **podcast about the orchestra** rather than performance, so it is excluded by the same rule as the anniversary video. Because the orchestra already published, **linking costs no permission and no bandwidth** — see [Decide how performance audio and video are hosted](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-27/decide-how-performance-audio-and-video-are-hosted). Of the 15 most recent uploads: 4 non-performance, 3 the "BSO Mexico 2020" batch (the 2020-02-23 program repeated on tour), 5 more from the same 2021-04 tour-era batch still unmatched, 2 the same in-scope performance published at two granularities, and 1 provably in scope (`pi-20221218-3`). The channel RSS feed (`youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=UCsDWWl_zTBj3K2_dFH6HMdg`) is readable without JavaScript, which the channel page itself is not.
* **All 13 halls are Brooklyn/NYC, so the archive holds no tour venue at all.** The source spreadsheet recorded the concert series and never the tour dates — which is the general form of the Mexico 2020 problem: any tour recording needs its concert entry created before it has anywhere to hang. Corollary for `attended` seeding: a tour date is net-new data of exactly the shape ADR-0006 anticipated for pre-BSO youth orchestras.
* The 3 undateable concerts (rows 7, 217, 266) are 1981–83 — all pre-tenure.
* **Alex appears nowhere in the archive.** No "Kale" among the 310 soloists, 37 conductors or 27 ensembles — confirming from the other direction that section players are unrecorded. Participation is therefore 100% net-new data, not an extension of anything already there.
* `programItem.work` **is populated on all 404 in-scope pairs.** Attribution is total; there are no composer-only program items in scope. Extends [AWK-15](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-15/settle-composer-identity-and-how-arrangements-are-modeled)'s finding that `programItem.composer` is null on all 384.
* `programItem` **entry ids are positional** — `pi-<date>-<index>`, derived from the concert id — and **a run's second night carries the first night's ids**: `cnc-20070523` links `pi-20070520-*`. So 20 items across the 7 runs would be misattributed by anything inferring a date from an id prefix, and the pending re-import **renumbers** items on those concerts.
* **In-scope organizations are effectively two, not three.** 125 BSO links + 1 BHO + 1 concert with no orchestra recorded (2007-12-16, the ditto case). **BHMS appears in zero in-scope concerts** — it is pre-tenure only.
* **Played-only facet cardinalities are far friendlier than archive-wide:** conductors 16 (of 37), halls 5 (of 13), seasons 25 (of 52), soloists 144 (of 310), ensembles 6 (of 27), genres 14 present (of 17). Program sizes run 1–10 items, mode 3.
* **Repertoire repeats are modest:** of the 348 in-scope works, **294 were played once, 52 twice, 2 three times**. This is why participation rules have to be evaluated per (concert, item) pair rather than per work.
* `bso-graph.json` **has been regenerated since the** `shares` **fix**, so it is no longer behind the checklist — its `shared_program` report lists all **8** runs and it reconciles to 127 / 384 / 404 / 348 / 173 exactly. Only **live Contentful** still holds the split programs.

## Contentful: the space and its schema

* `scripts/contentful/bso-graph.json` is **parser output, not Contentful state** — but as of [Audit the Contentful space](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-9/audit-the-contentful-space) the live state is known and the two agree on every count **except** `hall` — the graph has 12, Contentful has 13, the extra being the row-912 orphan `hal-grand-street-campus-high-schools`, which has never appeared in parser output. The Contentful MCP's Cloudflare 1101 is **intermittent, not cleared** — on 2026-07-31 it threw once and then succeeded on retry inside a single session, so a 1101 is worth exactly one retry despite Cloudflare's "do not retry" text. No CMA token is needed, and none is configured locally.
* **Entry ids follow two conventions.** Importer-created entries have derived ids (`cnc-20260614`, `wrk-…`, `cmp-…`, `hal-…`); entries hand-created before the importer kept Contentful's auto-generated ids — the 8 dated composers, **Brooklyn Symphony Orchestra** (`7GW6fT7mUQE6pZLJXHj1TC`), and **Walt Whitman Hall** (`4ej6X1ysgy6FlHGz7QsQYX`). Anything keying on an id prefix silently misses these, and one of them is the BSO itself.
* **The whole space holds 1 asset.** Portfolio imagery lives only in the repo's `assets/images/`; nothing has been migrated.
* **The space had zero RichText fields and zero Asset-link fields** before the portfolio types. Every text field across all 11 archive types is `Symbol` or `Text`, and no content type referenced a media asset anywhere — *which is why the space holds exactly 1 asset.* There was never a field capable of holding one. See [Design the content model for design/dev work](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-11/design-the-content-model-for-designdev-work).
* **Exactly one Contentful environment exists** — `master`, created 2019-05-19. Environment-based partitioning was never available without creating one.
* **Field IDs in Contentful are scoped per content type**, so cross-type field-name collisions are impossible; only content-type IDs and entry IDs are space-global. Entry-id prefixes (`cmp-`, `cnc-`, `wrk-`) exist only because a script created those entries — hand-authored entries take Contentful's auto-generated ids and need no prefix convention.
* **The space already uses both taxonomy patterns:** controlled `in`-validated symbols (`soloist.instrument` 36 values, `ensemble.kind` 7) *and* linked single-field entities (`genre`, 17 entries whose only field is `name`). Either has precedent; pick per case.
* `concert.orchestra` **is an Array of links in the live schema**, though `bso-graph.json` treats it as a single value in places. Matters to anything reading concerts from the CDA.
* ~~**None of the decided schema exists in Contentful yet**~~ — **the archive half now does.** AWK-30 applied it on 2026-08-14: `concert.attended`/`satOut`, `composer.slug`/`period`, `conductor.slug` and `work.period`/`forms`/`arranger`/`arrangementType`/`arrangementOf` are all live on `master` and activated, verified by `sys.publishedVersion`. **The `project` type is still absent** and has no migration. Two fields deliberately did *not* change and are the next reader's trap: `work.genre` still exists (it goes only after AWK-37 migrates it to `forms`) and `work.slug` still carries `unique: true` (it comes off only after AWK-39's `(composer, slug)` assertion, per ADR-0008). Re-check with `python3 scripts/contentful/migrate_schema.py --dry-run` rather than trusting this line.
* **Schema existing is not data existing, and not a wired CDA.** Every field AWK-30 added is *empty* — it wrote no entry data at all — and no CDA token is configured locally. So the prerender still runs from `bso-graph.json` + `participation-checklist.md`, and AWK-36/37/39 are what close the gap. Anything reading a new field from the Delivery API today gets nothing, which looks identical to the field not existing.
* **`work.forms` is `Array<Symbol>`, not `Array<Link genre>`.** ADR-0007's schema block said Link and was corrected under AWK-30; its 25 values are the 17 `genre` entry *names* plus ADR-0007's 8 additions. So the `genre` type is now vocabulary-only and loses its last reader when AWK-37 runs. Extending the vocabulary means editing the `in` list in `scripts/contentful/archive-schema.json`, not creating an entry.

## Netlify, DNS and the cutover

* **The Netlify account is on a frozen *Legacy Free* plan** — created 2018-10-08, long before the 2025-09-04 credit-based cutover, and legacy is the no-action default. 300 build min/month, 100 GB bandwidth, **1 concurrent build**, and on *Free* specifically both limits are **hard stops rather than overages** (Starter/Pro buy out at $7/500 min; Free cannot). Requests are not metered on legacy at all. Migration to credits is opt-in and **irreversible**, and would retighten the allowance to ~20 production deploys/month — so the design must not *depend* on minutes being the meter. See [Confirm what Netlify actually meters, and what throttles deploys](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-16/confirm-what-netlify-actually-meters-and-what-throttles-deploys).
* **The split-DNS arrangement ADR-0002 reasoned about is already running in production on this exact zone.** `starwars.awkale.me` CNAMEs to a live Netlify project while `awkale.me` keeps Namecheap nameservers and all five MX records — verified live. Pointing records at Netlify without breaking `hi@awkale.me` is demonstrated, not theoretical.
* **The apex currently resolves to GitHub Pages A records** — `185.199.108–111.153`, four of them. The cutover is concretely *remove those four, then add one ALIAS*. **Namecheap's free BasicDNS does support ALIAS at the apex** — its docs name `BasicDNS`, `FreeDNS` and `PremiumDNS` alike, at no extra cost — so ADR-0002's open question resolves in favour of the preferred path and its hardcoded-IP fallback (`75.2.60.5`) is **not needed**. Netlify's ALIAS target is the hostname `apex-loadbalancer.netlify.com`, not an IP. Namecheap **requires** conflicting `A` / `AAAA` / `CNAME` records be removed before an ALIAS can be created on the same host, so delete-then-add is the vendor's own precondition rather than DNS pedantry. ALIAS TTL is capped at **1 or 5 minutes**, which usefully bounds the cutover rollback window.
* **Cost of apex-canonical on external DNS, unrecorded until now:** Netlify's own guidance is that an apex on third-party DNS *"can't take advantage of direct DNS routing on a global CDN like Netlify's"*, and it recommends a subdomain as the primary hostname when external DNS is in play. That conflicts with ADR-0001's apex-canonical commitment — but the conflict is **not resolvable**, since Namecheap's email forwarding pins the zone and twelve redirects plus the existing certificate assume the apex. It is a performance cost to accept knowingly, not a decision to revisit.
* **Landmine — the Netlify UI defaults into the employer's team.** The user's `preferred_account_id` is `dv01` (a Pro team, 12 members, Alex also Owner), not the personal `awkale` team (`5bbb5d8f67610c37be6d7ea8`). Creating the new site without deliberately switching teams lands a personal site on an employer's paid account — wrong billing, access and ownership, and painful to unpick once DNS and TLS are attached. **Decided 2026-08-02: the personal** `awkale` **team.** That makes this a footgun to verify at site-creation time rather than an open question.
* `awkale.netlify.app` **is already taken** by a same-team project named `awkale` that has never successfully deployed (`currentDeploy` empty). ADR-0002 assumes staging lives at `<site>.netlify.app`. **Decided 2026-08-02: reclaim it** — the dead `awkale` project is reconnected to the new `awkale/awkale.me` GitHub repo rather than a new project being created, which keeps the `awkale.netlify.app` staging URL and costs nothing, since it has never deployed. Six personal-team projects exist: `awkale`, `awkale-starwars`, `bso`, `uxstash`, `agent-a`, `brave-swirles-c3e5f7`.
* **The Netlify MCP is authenticated as Alex** (`hi@awkale.me`, GitHub `awkale`), so team, plan and project state are all readable AFK. It exposes **no billing or usage surface** — that, and only that, still needs a human at the billing page.
* **Build minutes are ~0.4 min/build, so the 300-minute hard stop is ~750 builds/month** — wider than [Confirm what Netlify actually meters, and what throttles deploys](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-16/confirm-what-netlify-actually-meters-and-what-throttles-deploys)'s 100–300 projection, which assumed 1–3 min. That ticket's conclusion and its standing caution are unchanged; only the margin is larger.
* **Netlify's metered limits are not one failure mode — forms stop the site serving, build minutes do not.** [Confirm what Netlify actually meters, and what throttles deploys](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-16/confirm-what-netlify-actually-meters-and-what-throttles-deploys) established build minutes as a hard stop; the legacy billing docs are explicit that it is **the gentle case**: *"If the build limit is reached, your sites will still be served to visitors, but new builds will be disabled for all sites on your account. For all other metered features, when reaching 100% of the limit, new builds will be disabled and **all sites on your account will be paused**."* Plus *"deleting the site with overages will not restore the account"*, and **free-tier accounts get no automatic upgrade** to a paid add-on level — they are simply blocked. So **Forms' 100 submissions/month is a remotely operable outage for all six personal-team projects**, not a bill and not a degraded form. Bounded by **spam-flagged submissions not consuming the allowance** (Akismet runs by default; only Verified submissions count, so it takes ~100 messages that *pass* Akismet, not 100 POSTs), warnings at 50/75/90%, and a one-click disable that needs no deploy. Accepted by [Decide whether anything needs a server](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-26/decide-whether-anything-needs-a-server) as an operational risk, since rate limiting would need the function that decision exists to avoid. **Generalize the shape, not just the number:** any metered feature this project adopts wants checking against this asymmetry before it is assumed to fail like build minutes do.
* **Form detection is a per-site Netlify UI toggle with no** `netlify.toml` **equivalent** — the first piece of this project's configuration living outside the repo, cutting against ADR-0002's config-as-code rationale for choosing Netlify at all. It belongs on the site-creation checklist beside the `awkale.netlify.app` reclaim: forget it and the form silently accepts nothing, with no build error and no deploy warning. **Related, and the first of its kind here: the deployed HTML stops equalling the built HTML** on the two contact pages, since post-processing strips `data-netlify` / `netlify-honeypot` and injects `form-name`. [Spike the 637-route prerender build](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-17/spike-the-637-route-prerender-build)'s CI assertion checks the page *set* rather than page *contents*, so nothing collides — but a diff between `build/client` and what the browser receives is now expected on exactly those two pages.

## Build, prerender and the framework

* **Prerender at this scale is a solved problem with ~41× headroom, and** `concurrency` **is a non-lever.** 594 routes build in **22 s on Netlify's own builder** (install + build + assert + upload of 1,201 files) and 5.6–6.6 s on a 2-vCPU/8 GB Linux runner at 314–359 MB peak RSS. The `prerender.concurrency` default is `1` — fully sequential — so the worry that default parallelism might overwhelm the build container was **backwards**; there is no aggressive default, raising it to 14 buys ~1 s, and peak RSS is flat across the sweep. Prerender is only ~1.2 s of the ~6 s build; the Vite bundle dominates. See [Spike the 637-route prerender build](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-17/spike-the-637-route-prerender-build).
* **A trailing slash in the** `prerender` **path list is a hard build failure**, not a redirect: React Router matches the route as `/projects` and refuses to SSR `/projects/`. **This resolves rather than contradicts ADR-0001** — Netlify 301s `/concerts` → `/concerts/`, so the ADR's slash-ful form is the canonical *served* address while the slash-free form is the *prerender input*. Two layers, both correct; only the enumerator needs to know. **Confirmed live on 2026-08-02, and it applies to *every* route rather than just the indexes** — the build emits `<path>/index.html` throughout, so `/concerts/2016-12-18` 301s to `/concerts/2016-12-18/` exactly as `/concerts` does. **Alex has confirmed he wants trailing slashes, so this is now the intended canonical form** — which makes **ADR-0001 the inconsistent record**, since it writes indexes slash-ful (`/projects/`, `/concerts/`) but leaves slash-free (`/concerts/2008-12-13`); the build is uniformly slash-ful and closer to the intent than the ADR's own notation. **Build instruction that is cheap now and tedious later: write every internal** `<Link to>` **with the trailing slash**, or each one costs a needless 301 hop on fresh load across ~590 pages. The `.data` hydration sidecars are slash-free, unredirected, and sit as siblings of the directory (`bolero.data` beside `bolero/index.html`), so the namespaces do not collide — **but 7.18 warns that v8 changes data-request URL formats** (`future.v8_trailingSlashAwareDataRequests`), so that flag is worth adopting early, which fits [AWK-8](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-8/choose-the-static-rendering-layer-above-vite)'s incremental-flag posture and is the one place the trailing-slash commitment touches the upgrade path.
* **A missing page 404s in production — it does not serve a silent shell.** [AWK-8](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-8/choose-the-static-rendering-layer-above-vite)'s `HydrateFallback` landmine needs a **catch-all redirect** to `__spa-fallback.html` to become invisible; with no such rule, unknown paths get a real 404 (verified live). **So: never add a catch-all redirect** — it is the redirect, not the framework, that would turn loud 404s into blank hydration shells across the whole archive. The single most valuable thing this spike found.
* **Two config traps.** `prerender.concurrency: undefined` is **rejected** — the validator tests the key's *presence*, not its value, so it must be omitted entirely to get the default (spread conditionally, never assign). And `@react-router/node` **is a required dependency even with** `ssr: false`, because prerendering still renders server-side at build time; [AWK-8](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-8/choose-the-static-rendering-layer-above-vite) does not list it.
* **Netlify's static path works despite being undocumented.** It auto-detected `framework: "react-router"` and served `build/client` from a hand-written `netlify.toml` with zero functions — the recipe gap [AWK-8](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-8/choose-the-static-rendering-layer-above-vite) flagged is a documentation gap, not a capability gap.
* **React Router 8.3.0 is current**; 7.18.2 remains the latest 7.x. [AWK-8](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-8/choose-the-static-rendering-layer-above-vite)'s pin and its "move to v8 once #15350 closes" posture are untouched by the spike, but the v8 line is live and the gap is widening.
* **React Router is on 8.3.0, and that was decided deliberately — this map was the stale record, not the code.** ADR-0009 carries a dedicated section, *"Version: 8.3.0, not the 7.18.x originally pinned"*: the pin **was dropped on 2026-08-03 and the exit condition was not met** — it was *superseded rather than satisfied*, because `bun create react-router` now scaffolds `react-router: ^8` / `@react-router/dev: ^8` / `vite: ^8` by default, so 7.18.x had become the off-path choice and the pin's rationale (avoid riding new surface area) stopped describing reality. ADR-0009 is honest about the two residual costs: **[#15350](https://github.com/remix-run/react-router/issues/15350) was never checked** — abandoned rather than cleared, though the site has no base path so it is very likely irrelevant — and [**Spike the 637-route prerender build**](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-17/spike-the-637-route-prerender-build)**'s figures were all taken on 7.18.x, so they are "indicative, not current"** against ~41× headroom. **Re-verified on 8.3.0:** `prerender` + `ssr: false` still emits real HTML per route, an unknown path still 404s rather than serving a shell, and `future.v8_trailingSlashAwareDataRequests` is now moot. Likewise `react-aria-components` **is deliberate and already recorded** — ADR-0004's *second* amendment reverses its own premise, dropping shadcn/ui as the component source in favour of React Aria styled in plain CSS keyed on data attributes, with Tailwind kept for layout. **A ticket was opened on 2026-08-03 to reconcile all this and immediately closed as mis-premised** ([Reconcile the React Router version](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-29/reconcile-the-react-router-version-the-pin-says-718x-the-code-says-v8)) — the map had simply never been updated from the commits. **The lesson is about the map, not the stack:** the repo moved faster than its index, so a fact bullet asserting a version is worth checking against `package.json` before it is trusted.
* [awk17-prerender-spike.netlify.app](https://awk17-prerender-spike.netlify.app)**now 404s at root**, so the *"delete both throwaways"* instruction below is partly discharged already. Whether the Netlify project record and the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/awkale/awk17-prerender-spike) still exist is unchecked.

## Analytics, headers and images

* **Universal Analytics is not merely dead, it is deleted.** Google removed UA properties, their data **and** the API the week of **2024-07-01**, inaccessible even read-only, with no export path afterwards — so `UA-171213-13` holds nothing recoverable and there is no property left to delete. `analytics.js` nonetheless still returns **200 (52 KB)**, so the retired site ships a script that silently discards every hit: a no-op, not a broken request. **Clicky, by contrast, is alive** — `clicky.com` → 200, `static.getclicky.com/js` → 200 (12.5 KB) — so site `100850507` plausibly holds **~2016–2026, the only surviving analytics history for** `awkale.me`. Both dispositions settled by [Decide whether the new site carries analytics](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-24/decide-whether-the-new-site-carries-analytics).
* **The old analytics tags were end-of-**`<body>`**, not** `<head>` — `_layouts/default.html:19` includes `_includes/scripts.html` after the content. Several tickets described this as a `<head>` decision; it never was.
* **The site's only client-side persistence is** `localStorage`**, and there are no cookies at all.** `app/lib/mode.ts` writes `theme` and `mode`; ADR-0004 already serves fonts from the site's own origin. First-party and functional, which is why [Decide whether the new site carries analytics](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-24/decide-whether-the-new-site-carries-analytics) could settle consent by construction rather than as a preference.
* `public/_headers` **exists and carries no** `Content-Security-Policy` — verified repo-wide. It holds the staging `noindex` (marked *REMOVE AT CUTOVER*) and immutable font caching, nothing else. So every "no third-party origin" property this map asserts was, until ADR-0010, a convention rather than a constraint.
* **Contentful's Free plan pauses delivery at 100%, so it is a second vendor with the same failure shape as Netlify.** 50 GB asset bandwidth and 100K API calls per month, *no overages*, and Contentful's changelog is explicit that reaching either limit **automatically pauses the delivery APIs** (CDA, CPA, GraphQL) until the next calendar month or a paid upgrade. **Outage, not invoice** — the third instance of the asymmetry [Confirm what Netlify actually meters, and what throttles deploys](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-16/confirm-what-netlify-actually-meters-and-what-throttles-deploys) taught this map to look for, after build minutes and Forms, and **the first on a vendor other than Netlify.** Generalize it: any free tier this project leans on wants checking against this shape before its limit is assumed to bill rather than break. Decisive in [Decide how Contentful asset images are delivered](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-28/decide-how-contentful-asset-images-are-delivered).
* **Netlify Image CDN is on by default, fail-closed, loud, and unmetered on legacy Free** — verified live on `uxstash` and `agent-a`, both static and zero-function on the same frozen plan. `/.netlify/images` answers from `Netlify Edge` with `{"code":400,"msg":"must provide the url param"}` on sites that opted into nothing, so **there is no per-site toggle** — the direct opposite of ADR-0011's Forms detection, which is UI-only and silent when forgotten, and therefore nothing here joins the site-creation checklist. An un-allowlisted source returns `400 "url (…) is not an allowed pattern"`, so omitting the config cannot degrade into an accidental hotlink. Image transformations appear nowhere on the legacy metered list (build minutes, bandwidth, function and edge-function invocations, forms). **Two costs:** transformed bytes now spend Netlify's 100 GB rather than Contentful's 50 GB — both hard stops — and `/.netlify/images` **is Netlify-only**, so a hosting move breaks every image URL on the site unless they are built in one helper module.
* `sizes="auto"` **requires** `loading="lazy"` **by specification, and Safari does not support it** — desktop or iOS, through 26.5, **blocked from Baseline since April 2026** on WebKit bug 253143; Chrome/Edge 126+, Firefox 150+ (April 2026), ~71% globally. So the hand-written `sizes` fallback is what serves Safari *today* rather than legacy politeness, and a design portfolio's audience skews Safari. Deleting the fallbacks is the whole migration when WebKit ships. The lazy requirement is why eager above-the-fold images keep an explicit `sizes`.
* **Images are a portfolio-only concern — the 590 archive pages carry none.** [Decide how performance audio and video are hosted](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-27/decide-how-performance-audio-and-video-are-hosted) made recordings outbound text links and rejected thumbnails, and the archive was never illustrated, so the entire image question touches the home page, `/projects` and the two case studies — around twenty files. Eight sit in the retired repo's `assets/images` (1.1 MB total, 2560px `@2x` PNGs plus two JPEGs), of which [Decide which Projects ship, and which get case studies](https://linear.app/awkale/issue/AWK-21/decide-which-projects-ship-and-which-get-case-studies) needs two as Cision `coverImage`s. Emit `width`/`height` from the CDA's `file.details.image.*` or each one costs a layout shift for nothing.
